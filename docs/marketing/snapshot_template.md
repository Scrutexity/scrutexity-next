# The Scrutexity Operating Model

**Claim gap → Visibility gap → Proof gap → Follow-up gap → Revenue gap**

Scrutexity finds the gap between what a business says, what buyers can verify, what AI/search can understand, and what demand the business is losing.

---

# Refined Free 3-Point Visibility & Trust Snapshot

Use this framework when delivering the free snapshot. It creates urgency through operational gaps, without overstating the power of AI or sounding like a generic SEO audit.

## 1. Claim / Trust Gap

**Claim found on your site:**
"[insert claim]"

**What I noticed:**
This is a strong buyer-facing claim, but I couldn't quickly find supporting proof near it — such as a case study, review, credential, metric, location-specific example, provider explanation, or structured FAQ that reinforces the claim.

**Why it matters:**
When a claim is not supported close to where it appears, buyers may be less likely to trust it. It can also make it harder for search and AI systems to confidently understand what the business should be known for.

**Quick fix:**
Add a proof block directly under the claim with:
- one specific example
- one review or testimonial
- one credential or provider detail
- one short FAQ answer
- one clear service/location context line

*Example copy:*
"[Business Name] provides [service] for [audience/location]. Our team supports this through [credential/process/proof point]. Patients/clients commonly choose us for [specific reason], reflected in [review/testimonial/proof]."

## 2. AI / Search Visibility Gap

**Prompt or buyer question tested:**
"[insert prompt or search-style question]"

**What I noticed:**
Your site does not clearly answer this question in a direct, structured way. The page may mention the service, but it does not make the answer easy enough for buyers, search engines, or AI systems to extract.

One pattern I noticed is that competitors who are easier to surface often have clearer service pages, FAQ-style answers, stronger review signals, and more structured proof around the exact buyer question.

**Why it matters:**
AI/search systems tend to rely on clear service definitions, FAQs, reviews, local context, proof signals, and consistent language across the site.

**Quick fix:**
Add a short FAQ block to the relevant service page.

*Example FAQ:*
Question: "Do you offer [service] in [city/location]?"
Answer: "Yes. [Business Name] offers [service] for [audience] in [location]. The service is designed for [specific use case], and clients/patients typically choose it when they need [buyer problem]."

## 3. Demand / Conversion Gap

**What I noticed:**
[Insert observed issue: unclear CTA, slow form path, no booking path, no follow-up language, weak review CTA, no recovery path, no missed inquiry process.]

**Why it matters:**
More visibility only helps if the business can convert and recover the demand it already receives. If calls, forms, consults, or review opportunities are not handled consistently, new traffic can leak before it becomes revenue.

**Quick fix:**
Add one clear next-step CTA and one recovery path.

*Example:*
Primary CTA: "Request a Consultation"
Recovery CTA: "Already reached out? Text us here and our team will follow up."
